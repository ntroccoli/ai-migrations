# Q12 guide: How will you measure cost and result?

For question 12 of the [checklist](../checklist.md): read it before writing up a finding on that
question. It walks through what the cases show; the project's owner decides. Each point names its
evidence: a lesson ID (its strength, its cases and the cases against it are in
[lessons/evidence.md](../lessons/evidence.md); its page links the case sections) or a case link.
"(ours)" marks the maintainer's reading, evidence level `ours`; so does a lesson tagged `[ours]` there,
which is our inference from its cases.

## Lessons behind it

- [CST-1](../lessons/cost.md): long runs stayed affordable only with high prompt-cache reuse.
- [CST-2](../lessons/cost.md), [CST-6](../lessons/cost.md), [CST-7](../lessons/cost.md): the full
  bill, the work after merge, and review agents as a multiple of generation.
- [MSR-1](../lessons/measurement.md), [MSR-2](../lessons/measurement.md): savings against a
  manual effort that never ran are self-estimates; every rate needs its base.
- [MSR-4](../lessons/measurement.md), [MSR-5](../lessons/measurement.md): gains separated from
  other work in the same window; known regressions published as a floor.
- [MSR-9](../lessons/measurement.md), [MSR-10](../lessons/measurement.md): people's share measured
  from day one; owners who rejected a change and migrated anyway counted.
- [TST-9](../lessons/testing.md): performance treated as part of the contract.
- [KNW-3](../lessons/knowledge.md): structured session logs from the start.

Cases, in our words: GitHub benchmarked before and after (amid other changes) and gave its token
bill; Bun and ts-rust priced tokens at list rates (ts-rust's restart used flat-rate plans); Bun
counted regressions after merge; Google measured shares as edit distance. Every effort or saving
figure is the team's own estimate. Bloomberg also counted undecided pull requests, and owners'
migrations that reused rejected ones. Recording reverts is ours.

## What it looks like in a project

- A manual estimate with no record of how it was made, so no saving can be measured against it
  (MSR-1).
- No baseline of the number the product exists for (speed, latency, memory, quality) on fixed
  hardware before the port ([GitHub](../cases/2026-09-16-github-copilot-runtime-rust.md#numbers), TST-9).
- Token spend known only from the bill, not per run or per unit (CST-1, KNW-3).

## Questions to ask of this project

- What is the manual estimate, and how was it made?
- What number does the product exist for, and is it measured on fixed hardware today?
- Where will tokens or spend, people's hours, pull requests, regressions after merge and reverts be
  recorded?
- Which figures will be measured, and which estimated?

## Trade-offs

- Logging tokens and hours from the start costs a little setup; in one case such logs later
  answered where the time went, and a bill rebuilt afterwards rests on estimates (KNW-3, MSR-9).
- A benchmark amid other changes credits the move with gains it did not make; separating them costs
  extra runs (MSR-4).

## When not to apply it

- A small personal move needs no cost study; the result number and the regressions still tell
  whether it was worth it (ours).
- Published costs from other cases rarely transfer: different models, prices and units (CST-4,
  CST-5).
